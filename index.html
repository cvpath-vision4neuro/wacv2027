Vision for Biomedical Discovery

CVPath+Vision4Neuro

One day, two communities. CVPath brings computer vision to computational and digital pathology; Vision4Neuro brings it to neural decoding, behaviour and clinical neuroscience. Each track keeps its own call for papers and reviewing, and they meet in a joint panel and a combined poster session on what the two fields genuinely share: foundation and multimodal models, robustness under real distribution shift, and evidence of clinical readiness.

Shared ground

Why these two workshops share a day

Tissue slides and neural recordings could hardly be more different as data. The methodological questions, however, line up closely — which is exactly what the joint panel and the combined poster session are for.

Foundation and multimodal models

Self-supervised pretraining on unlabelled data, vision-language alignment, and generative models are reshaping both fields at once — on gigapixel tissue and on neural signals alike.

Robustness and generalization

Scanner, stain and centre shift in pathology; cross-subject, cross-session and cross-device shift in neural decoding. Both need calibration, open-set awareness and honest evaluation protocols.

Clinical translation

What actually counts as evidence that a model is ready to touch patient care? The joint panel puts pathologists, neuroscientists and vision researchers in the same room on that question.

An interpretability benchmark for hypothesis-free discovery in prostate cancer histopathology. For authors

Frequently asked questions

Will my paper be published in the WACV 2027 proceedings?

Only regular papers. Accepted 8-page regular papers are archival and appear in the WACV 2027 Workshop Proceedings on IEEE Xplore and in the CVF Open Access repository. Short papers (4 pages) and extended abstracts (2 pages) are non-archival: they are presented at the workshop but are not published, so the work remains free for submission elsewhere.

Which track should I submit to?

Submit to the community best placed to review your work. CVPath covers computational and digital pathology; Vision4Neuro covers neural decoding, behaviour and clinical neuroscience. Each track has its own program committee, and you select the track when you submit. If a paper genuinely spans both, write to either primary contact and we will agree on the better fit.

Are the two tracks reviewed together?

No. Each track runs its own reviewing process with its own program committee and criteria. The shared parts of the day are the opening, the panel, the poster session, the awards and the website.

What paper format should I use?

The official WACV 2027 author kit, in LaTeX or Word, the same template as the main conference. Page limits exclude references, and supplementary material does not count towards the limit. Reviewing is double-blind, so submissions must be anonymised.

Can I present remotely?

No. WACV 2027 does not accommodate virtual workshops or remote presentations. At least one author of each accepted contribution must register for the conference and present in person at Disney Springs.

Can I submit work that is under review elsewhere?

Regular papers must be original and not substantially similar to work under review or published elsewhere, following the WACV dual-submission policy. Because short papers and extended abstracts are non-archival, they may cover work that is concurrently under review at another venue, provided the policy of that venue allows it.

How do I take part in the OWL challenge?

The challenge runs on the Grand Challenge platform, within the CVPath track. Registration and the phase dates are published on the challenge page. Taking part does not require submitting a paper, and top-ranking teams present their methods during the challenge session.

Do I have to attend the whole day?

No, but the day is designed to reward it: the joint panel after lunch and the single combined poster session are where the two communities actually meet.
