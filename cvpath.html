Scope and motivation

Computational pathology has largely grown inside the medical imaging community (MICCAI, ISBI, COMPAY). With pathology foundation models now openly available, the field has become accessible to computer vision researchers without clinical data access — and its core problems are computer vision problems: gigapixel understanding, real domain shift, weak supervision, dense prediction at cellular scale.

With its applications-first focus, WACV is an ideal venue to build ties with a scientific domain still under-represented at the conference, complementing rather than duplicating existing medical imaging workshops. We expect the track to increase the visibility of open pathology problems within computer vision, accelerate the adoption of state-of-the-art vision methods in pathology, and — through the OWL challenge — deliver a public benchmark for interpretable AI evaluated by both AI researchers and clinical experts.

CVPath hosts the OWL challenge. Observing What Models Learn benchmarks the interpretability of models predicting prostate cancer biochemical recurrence, on 1,419 prostatectomy cases from three medical centres, with Dockerized submissions on the Grand Challenge platform. Read the challenge page.
Call for papers

Topics of interest

We invite original research contributions in, but not limited to, the following areas.

  • Foundation and self-supervised models for pathology
  • Whole-slide image analysis and gigapixel understanding
  • Cell- and nuclei-level vision; dense prediction at cellular scale
  • Imaging beyond H&E: IHC, multiplex / mIF, multi-stain
  • Robustness, domain shift and weak supervision in computational pathology
  • Multimodal, generative and vision-language models in computational pathology
  • AI interpretability and trustworthy AI in computational pathology
  • Reproducibility, benchmarks, deployment and clinical translation

Submission instructions

How to submit to CVPath

  1. Prepare your paper in the WACV 2027 format

    Use the official WACV 2027 author kit (LaTeX or Word), the same template as the main conference. Page limits above exclude references; supplementary material is optional and is not counted in the page limit.

  2. Anonymise the submission

    Reviewing is double-blind for all submission types. Remove author names and affiliations, and avoid identifying acknowledgements or self-references in the first person.

  3. Submit through OpenReview, choosing the CVPath track

    Both tracks share one submission site with a track selector, so that authors of either half of the day use the same entry point.

  4. Present in person

    WACV 2027 does not accommodate virtual workshops or remote presentations. At least one author of every accepted contribution must register for the conference and present on site at Disney Springs.
