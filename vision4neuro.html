Scope and motivation

Vision4Neuro is about computer vision for neural decoding, behaviour and clinical neuroscience. Reconstructing what someone saw from EEG, MEG, fMRI, fNIRS or ECoG; aligning the representations of vision-language models with cortical responses; reading behaviour from video through eye tracking and markerless pose estimation; and turning any of this into interfaces and clinical measures that work outside a controlled lab.

The recurring obstacle is the same one WACV cares about: models that work on the subjects, sessions and devices they were fitted on, and degrade elsewhere. Cross-subject and cross-session transfer, domain shift, calibration and open-set recognition are therefore first-class topics of the track, not afterthoughts.

Shared with CVPath. The joint panel after lunch asks what counts as evidence that a model is ready for clinical use — a question with the same shape in neural decoding as in whole-slide imaging. Posters from both tracks are presented together in one combined session.
Call for papers

Topics of interest

We invite original research contributions in, but not limited to, the following areas.

  • Decoding and reconstruction of visual experience from neural signals (EEG, MEG, fMRI, fNIRS, ECoG)
  • Brain-to-image and brain-to-video generation
  • Encoding models and representational alignment between vision-language models and cortex
  • Multimodal neuroimaging and fusion
  • Brain-computer interfaces, gaze-driven interfaces and assistive communication
  • Vision-based behavioural phenotyping: eye tracking and markerless pose analysis
  • Clinical and translational applications in neurodevelopmental and neurodegenerative conditions
  • Robustness in the wild: cross-subject and cross-session transfer, domain shift, calibration, open-set recognition

Submission instructions

How to submit to Vision4Neuro

  1. Prepare your paper in the WACV 2027 format

    Use the official WACV 2027 author kit (LaTeX or Word), the same template as the main conference. Page limits above exclude references; supplementary material is optional and is not counted in the page limit.

  2. Anonymise the submission

    Reviewing is double-blind for all submission types. Remove author names and affiliations, and avoid identifying acknowledgements or self-references in the first person.

  3. Submit through OpenReview, choosing the Vision4Neuro track

    Both tracks share one submission site with a track selector, so that authors of either half of the day use the same entry point.

  4. Present in person

    WACV 2027 does not accommodate virtual workshops or remote presentations. At least one author of every accepted contribution must register for the conference and present on site at Disney Springs.

Questions on submission to this track: mpetrov@uccs.edu.
